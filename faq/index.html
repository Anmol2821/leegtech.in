What is the difference between a patent, trademark, copyright and design?

A patent protects an invention or technical innovation, a trademark protects your brand identity, copyright protects original creative works, and design registration protects the visual appearance of a product. One product can be protected by more than one of them.

Patent questions

What can be patented in India?

An invention can be patented in India if it is new, involves an inventive step and is capable of industrial application, and is not excluded under Sections 3 and 4 of the Patents Act, 1970. Exclusions include mere discoveries, abstract theories, business methods, methods of medical treatment, and computer programmes per se.

Learn more

How long is a patent valid in India?

A patent in India is valid for 20 years from the date of filing, provided annual renewal fees are paid from the third year onwards.

Learn more

How long does it take to get a patent in India?

It usually takes a few years from filing to grant, depending on the technology field, objections raised and the Patent Office workload. Eligible startups, small entities and some other applicants can request expedited examination to shorten this. No particular timeline can be guaranteed.

Learn more

What is the difference between a provisional and a complete patent application?

A provisional application secures an early filing date with a description of the invention and no claims; a complete specification fully describes the invention and includes claims. The complete specification must be filed within 12 months of the provisional application.

Learn more

What is the deadline to reply to a patent FER in India?

The reply to a First Examination Report must put the application in order within six months from the date of the FER, extendable by up to three months on request. Missing it leads to the application being treated as abandoned.

Learn more

Can I file a patent abroad directly from India?

Residents of India must either file in India first and wait six weeks, or obtain foreign filing permission under Section 39 of the Patents Act, before filing abroad. A PCT application filed with the Indian Patent Office counts as an Indian filing.

Learn more

Can software be patented in India?

Computer programmes per se cannot be patented in India, but an invention that produces a technical effect or technical contribution, with software as one part, may be patentable depending on how it is claimed.

Learn more

Design questions

What is design registration in India?

Design registration protects the visual appearance of a product, such as its shape, configuration, pattern, ornamentation or composition of lines and colours, under the Designs Act, 2000. It does not protect how the product works.

Learn more

How long does design registration last in India?

A registered design is protected for 10 years from the date of registration and can be extended once by 5 more years, giving a maximum of 15 years.

Learn more

Can I register a design that is already on sale?

Generally no. A design must be new or original and not previously published in India or elsewhere, so you should file before any public disclosure, sale or online listing.

Learn more

What is the difference between design registration and copyright?

Design registration protects the appearance of an industrial article, while copyright protects artistic works. Copyright in a design that is capable of registration can be lost once the article is reproduced more than 50 times by an industrial process without registration, so registration is the safer route for products.

Learn more

Does India have utility models?

No. India does not currently have a utility model system. Technical inventions are protected by patents in India; utility models are available in countries such as Germany.

Learn more

Trademark & Copyright questions

How long is a trademark valid in India?

A registered trademark in India is valid for 10 years from the date of application and can be renewed every 10 years indefinitely.

Learn more

How long does trademark registration take in India?

If there are no objections or oppositions, registration commonly takes several months to around a year or more, depending on Registry timelines. Objections, hearings and oppositions add time, and no specific timeline can be guaranteed.

Learn more

What is the difference between ™ and ®?

™ can be used with a mark you claim, including once you have applied. ® can be used only after the trademark is registered; falsely representing a mark as registered is an offence under the Trade Marks Act, 1999.

Learn more

What happens if my trademark application is objected?

You must reply to the examination report within one month. If the reply is not accepted, a hearing is scheduled. An objection is not a refusal, and many objected marks proceed to registration.

Learn more

How many classes should I file my trademark in?

File in every class covering the goods or services you offer now or plan to offer soon. India follows the Nice Classification of 45 classes: 34 for goods and 11 for services.

Learn more

Is copyright registration compulsory in India?

No. Copyright exists automatically once an original work is created, but registration gives prima facie evidence of the particulars recorded, which is useful in disputes and transactions.

Learn more

Can I register my trademark in other countries?

Yes. Based on your Indian application or registration, you can seek protection in many countries through the Madrid Protocol, or file directly in each country.

Learn more

Business & Tax questions

Which business structure is best for a startup in India?

A Private Limited Company usually suits startups that plan to raise equity investment or offer ESOPs. An LLP suits professional and owner-run businesses that want limited liability with lower compliance. The right choice depends on your plans.

Learn more

How many directors are needed for a private limited company?

A private limited company needs at least two directors and two shareholders (the same people can be both), and at least one director must be resident in India. A One Person Company needs only one member.

Learn more

Who is eligible for DPIIT Startup India recognition?

A private limited company, LLP or registered partnership firm that is up to 10 years old, has not had turnover above ₹100 crore in any financial year, and is working on innovation or a scalable business model can apply.

Learn more

When is GST registration mandatory?

GST registration is generally required when aggregate turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh for goods or ₹20 lakh for services in most states, and regardless of turnover in some cases, such as inter-state taxable supply of goods and most e-commerce sales.

Learn more

Is MSME (Udyam) registration free?

Yes. There is no government fee for Udyam registration on the official portal. Professional assistance is optional.

Learn more

What annual compliance does a private limited company have?

A private limited company must hold board meetings and an AGM, get its accounts audited, file financial statements (AOC-4) and its annual return (MGT-7 or MGT-7A) with the Registrar of Companies, and file its income tax return, among other filings.

Learn more

Legal questions

Is a non-compete clause enforceable in India?

Post-employment non-compete clauses are generally unenforceable in India because Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 makes agreements in restraint of trade void. Restrictions during employment, confidentiality and non-solicitation clauses are more likely to be upheld.

Learn more

Is an NDA legally binding in India?

Yes. An NDA is a contract under the Indian Contract Act, 1872 and is enforceable if it is validly made and properly stamped as per the relevant state's stamp law.

Learn more

Can agreements be signed electronically in India?

Most commercial agreements can be signed electronically under the Information Technology Act, 2000. Some documents are excluded, such as wills, trusts, powers of attorney, negotiable instruments and contracts for the sale of immovable property.

Learn more

Does an agreement need stamp duty?

Yes, most agreements attract stamp duty under the stamp law of the relevant state. An inadequately stamped document cannot be admitted in evidence until the duty and penalty are paid.

Learn more

When must a cheque bounce notice be sent?

Under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, the demand notice must be sent within 30 days of receiving information of the dishonour, and the drawer then has 15 days to pay.

Learn more

Are online agreement templates enough?

Templates are a starting point, but they often miss business-specific risks, Indian law points and enforceability requirements such as proper stamping. A tailored agreement is easier to rely on.

Learn more
