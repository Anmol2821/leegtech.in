Once a request for examination is filed, the Indian Patent Office examines the application and sends the applicant a First Examination Report (FER), formally the first statement of objections. How you respond decides whether the application moves towards grant. This guide explains the deadline, the usual objections and how to prepare a reply under the Patents Act, 1970 and the Patents Rules, 2003.

What is an FER?

After examination, the examiner's report is considered by the Controller, who then issues the first statement of objections to the applicant or their authorised agent (rule 24B). The FER lists every requirement the application does not yet meet, often with prior art documents cited against the claims.

The deadline: six months, extendable by three

Under Section 21(1), an application is deemed abandoned unless the applicant complies with all requirements within the prescribed period. Rule 24B(5) sets that period at six months from the date the first statement of objections is issued. Under rule 24B(6) it can be extended by three months, on a request in Form 4 with the prescribed fee, filed before the six months expire.

This is the period for putting the whole application in order for grant, not just for sending a first reply. Filing early leaves time to deal with any further objections within the same period. You can note your dates with our Patent Deadline Calculator.

Common objections in an FER

  • Lack of novelty: a cited document is said to disclose every feature of a claim.
  • Lack of inventive step: the claims are said to be obvious from one or more cited documents.
  • Non-patentable subject matter: the claims are said to fall within Section 3, for example a computer programme per se or a business method under Section 3(k).
  • Clarity and support: claims are unclear, too broad, or not fairly based on the description.
  • Sufficiency: the specification does not describe the invention well enough for a skilled person to work it.
  • Unity of invention: the claims cover more than one invention.
  • Formal requirements: documents, drawings, forms or details of corresponding foreign applications (Section 8) that need to be filed or corrected.

How to prepare the reply

  1. List every objection. Each one must be answered; an unanswered objection can be enough to keep the application from grant.
  2. Study the cited documents. Compare each claim feature by feature with the closest prior art and identify what is genuinely different.
  3. Decide between argument and amendment. Sometimes a technical explanation is enough. Often the stronger route is to narrow the claims so they clearly avoid the prior art.
  4. Keep amendments within the original disclosure. Under Section 59(1), amendments may only be by way of disclaimer, correction or explanation. The amended specification must not describe or claim matter not in substance disclosed before, and every amended claim must fall wholly within the scope of a claim before the amendment.
  5. Explain the technical advance. For inventive step, show the problem solved, why the cited documents do not lead to the solution, and any technical effect. Supporting data can help.
  6. Clear the formal points. File any required documents and corrected pages at the same time.
  7. Consider asking for a hearing. Section 80 entitles the applicant to be heard before the Controller decides against them, provided the request is made at least ten days before the deadline expires. A hearing request in the reply keeps that option open if objections remain.

What happens after the reply?

Replies are processed in the order they are received (rule 24B(4)). If the Controller is satisfied, the application proceeds to grant. If objections remain, a hearing is usually scheduled, after which written submissions are filed and the Controller decides. A hearing can be adjourned on request with reasonable cause, but no party gets more than two adjournments, each of not more than thirty days (rule 129A).

Frequently asked questions

Can I file the FER reply after six months?

Only if a Form 4 extension request was filed before the six months ran out. The extension is limited to three months. Otherwise the application is deemed abandoned under Section 21(1).

Can I add new features to the claims in my reply?

Only features already disclosed in the specification, and only so that each amended claim stays within the scope of an earlier claim (Section 59(1)). Adding new technical matter is not allowed.

Does every FER lead to a hearing?

No. If the reply overcomes all objections, the application can proceed to grant without a hearing.
