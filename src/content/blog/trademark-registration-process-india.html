Your brand name and logo are often the first assets your business creates, and among the most valuable. Registering them as trademarks gives you exclusive rights and the legal tools to act against copycats. This guide walks through each stage of trademark registration in India under the Trade Marks Act, 1999 and the Trade Marks Rules, 2017.

Step 1: Choose a strong, registrable mark

The Trade Marks Registry refuses marks on absolute grounds (Section 9), for example marks that are descriptive, generic, non-distinctive or deceptive. Invented or arbitrary words ("Kodak", "Apple" for computers) are the strongest. Descriptive words ("Best Rice" for rice) are the hardest to register.

Step 2: Run a trademark search

A mark can also be refused, opposed or challenged if it conflicts with an earlier identical or similar mark for similar goods or services (Section 11). A professional search checks for identical, phonetically similar and visually similar marks, and their current status, before you invest in branding.

Step 3: Identify the right classes

India follows the Nice Classification: 45 classes, of which classes 1 to 34 cover goods and 35 to 45 cover services. A software company may need Class 9 (downloadable software) and Class 42 (SaaS and IT services); a restaurant brand typically needs Class 43, and possibly Class 30 for packaged food products.

Step 4: File the application (Form TM-A)

The application is filed online with the Trade Marks Registry. You receive an application number immediately, and your filing date establishes your priority in India. From this point you may use the ™ symbol with your mark. Reduced official fees apply to individuals, startups and small enterprises.

If you are already using the mark, you can claim the date of first use, supported by an affidavit and evidence. Otherwise the application is filed on a "proposed to be used" basis.

Step 5: Examination

An examiner reviews the application and issues an examination report. If there are objections, under Section 9, Section 11 or on formal grounds, you must reply within one month of the report being communicated. Missing this deadline can lead to the application being treated as abandoned.

Step 6: Show-cause hearing (if needed)

If the Registry is not satisfied with the reply, it schedules a hearing, commonly conducted by video conference. Arguments, case law and evidence of use are presented, after which the Registrar decides whether to accept the application.

Step 7: Publication in the Trade Marks Journal

Once accepted, the mark is advertised in the Trade Marks Journal. This gives the public notice of your application.

Step 8: Opposition window

Any person may oppose the application within four months of the date of advertisement (Section 21). If an opposition is filed, you must file a counter-statement within two months of receiving the notice, otherwise the application is treated as abandoned. The case then proceeds through evidence and a hearing.

Step 9: Registration

If no opposition is filed, or an opposition is decided in your favour, the mark is registered and a registration certificate is issued. You may now use the ® symbol.

Step 10: Renewal every 10 years

Registration is valid for 10 years from the date of application and can be renewed every 10 years indefinitely (Section 25). Renewal can be filed up to six months before expiry, and within six months after expiry with a surcharge.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing a descriptive name that is hard to register and easy for others to use.
  • Skipping the search and discovering a conflicting mark after launch.
  • Filing in too few classes and leaving room for others in related areas.
  • Using ® before registration.
  • Missing the one-month objection reply or the counter-statement deadline.
  • Letting a freelance designer keep copyright in your logo: take a written assignment.

Frequently asked questions

Can I register a trademark without a company?

Yes. Individuals and sole proprietors can apply, and the mark can later be assigned to a company.

Does trademark registration protect my brand outside India?

No, trademark rights are territorial. You can extend protection to many countries through the Madrid Protocol based on your Indian application or registration. Learn about international trademark registration →
