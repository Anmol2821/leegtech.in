Owning a patent in India comes with a reporting duty that many patentees overlook: the statement of working, filed in Form 27. It tells the Patent Office whether the patented invention is being worked on a commercial scale in India. The Patents (Amendment) Rules, 2024 made the filing less frequent and the form simpler. This guide explains the current position.

The legal basis

Section 146(2) of the Patents Act, 1970 requires every patentee and every licensee, exclusive or otherwise, to furnish statements on the extent to which the patented invention has been worked on a commercial scale in India, in the manner, form and at the intervals prescribed. Rule 131(1) of the Patents Rules, 2003 prescribes Form 27, verified by the patentee, the licensee or an authorised agent.

Separately, under Section 146(1), the Controller can at any time ask a patentee or licensee by written notice for information on commercial working, to be given within two months or any further time the Controller allows.

When is Form 27 due?

Since the 2024 amendment, rule 131(2) requires the statement once for every period of three financial years, starting from the financial year that begins immediately after the financial year in which the patent was granted. It must be filed within six months from the end of each three-year period.

The Controller may condone a delay or extend the time by up to three months on a request in Form 4.

Before the 2024 amendment, the statement was required every financial year.

Example

If a patent was granted in the financial year 2024–25:

  • the first three-year period covers 2025–26, 2026–27 and 2027–28;
  • that period ends on 31 March 2028;
  • Form 27 for it is due within six months, that is by 30 September 2028.

What Form 27 asks

The form substituted by the 2024 Rules is short. It asks for:

  1. the name, address and nationality of the patentee or licensee, and the patent number(s);
  2. the period to which the statement relates;
  3. whether each patent is worked or not worked;
  4. if not worked, the reason, for example: under development or commercial trial, under review or approval with regulatory authorities, exploring commercial licensing, or any other reason specified;
  5. whether the patent is available for licensing and, if the patentee wishes, contact details for people interested in a licence.

The form states that it carries no fee. One form may cover several patents if they are related and granted to the same patentee(s). Where a patent is granted to two or more persons, the form may be filed jointly by all or any of them.

The form also notes that, subject to the conditions in the Act, a patented invention is not to be treated as "not worked" merely because the patented product has been imported into India.

Why it matters

  • Penalties: failing to furnish a statement required under Section 146 attracts a monetary penalty under Section 122(1), with a further daily penalty while the failure continues. Furnishing information known or believed to be false attracts a separate penalty under Section 122(2).
  • Compulsory licences: after three years from the grant of a patent, any person interested may apply for a compulsory licence on grounds that include the patented invention not being worked in the territory of India (Section 84(1)). Working information is relevant to such applications.
  • Public record: the Controller may publish the information received (Section 146(3)).

Practical tips

  • Record each patent's grant date and work out its three-year periods as soon as it is granted.
  • Licensees have their own duty to file; agree in the licence who prepares the statement.
  • Keep internal records of manufacture, sales, imports and licensing that support the statement.
  • Answer accurately. If a patent is not yet worked, choose the reason that reflects the actual position.

Frequently asked questions

Do I file Form 27 while my application is pending?

No. The duty applies to granted patents; the first period starts with the financial year after the year of grant.

Is there an official fee for Form 27?

No. The form itself states "No Fee". A Form 4 request for condonation or extension does have an official fee.

Can one Form 27 cover all my patents?

Only related patents granted to the same patentee(s) can be combined in one form.
