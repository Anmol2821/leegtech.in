One of the first questions in any trademark application is: which class should I file in? Choosing the wrong class, or too few classes, can leave your brand unprotected in the market where it matters most. This guide explains how trademark classes work in India and how to choose them sensibly.

What are trademark classes?

India follows the Nice Classification, an international system administered by WIPO that groups goods and services into 45 classes. Classes 1 to 34 cover goods, and classes 35 to 45 cover services. Every trademark application under the Trade Marks Act, 1999 must specify the goods or services and the classes they fall in.

Your registration protects the mark for the goods or services listed in the classes you file. In general, another business may be able to use a similar mark for unrelated goods or services, although well-known marks receive wider protection.

Step 1: List what you actually sell, and what you plan to sell

Write down every product and service you offer under the brand today, and anything you realistically plan to offer in the near future. Classification follows what the goods or services are, not the industry you think of yourself as being in.

Step 3: Check related classes

Many brands sell both goods and services. A fashion label that sells clothing (Class 25) through its own website may also want Class 35 for online retail services. A software company usually needs both Class 9 and Class 42. A food brand that runs outlets may need Class 43 as well as the relevant food classes.

Step 4: Run a trademark search in each class

Before filing, search for identical and similar marks in your chosen classes and closely related ones. The Registry can object to a mark that is similar to an earlier mark for similar goods or services under Section 11 of the Act. A search helps you avoid objections and opposition later.

Step 5: Decide between one application or several

India allows a single application covering multiple classes, with official fees charged for each class. Separate applications per class are also common, because a problem in one class then does not hold up the others. The right approach depends on your budget and risk.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Filing only in Class 35 for a product business: Class 35 covers services such as retail, not the products themselves.
  • Using vague descriptions that the examiner may object to. Describe your goods or services clearly.
  • Ignoring future expansion. Adding a new class later needs a new application with a later date.
  • Over-filing in classes you will never use. Registered marks that are not used for five years can be vulnerable to removal for non-use.

Frequently asked questions

How many classes are there for trademarks in India?

There are 45 classes under the Nice Classification: 34 for goods (Classes 1–34) and 11 for services (Classes 35–45).

Can I add a class after filing?

You cannot extend an existing application to a new class. You file a new application for the additional class.

Is the official fee charged per class?

Yes. The official fee is payable for each class, with reduced fees for individuals, startups and small enterprises.
