Choosing between a Private Limited Company and a Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) is one of the first legal decisions a founder makes. Both give limited liability and a separate legal identity, but they suit different goals. This guide compares them on the points that matter most.

When a Private Limited Company makes sense

A private company is usually the better choice if you plan to:

  • raise money from angel investors or venture capital funds;
  • give employees stock options (ESOPs);
  • build a brand that customers, banks and large clients see as a corporate entity;
  • scale the business and possibly list it in future.

The trade-off is more compliance: board meetings, an annual general meeting, a statutory audit, and annual filings with the Registrar of Companies. Late filings attract an additional fee for each day of delay.

When an LLP makes sense

An LLP is often a good fit for:

  • professional firms and consultancies;
  • family or owner-run businesses that do not plan equity fundraising;
  • partners who want flexibility to set profit sharing and management in an LLP agreement.

LLPs have lighter compliance. A statutory audit is required only when turnover or partners' contribution crosses the limits in the LLP Act. The two main annual filings are the Annual Return (Form 11), due by 30 May, and the Statement of Account & Solvency (Form 8), due by 30 October.

What about taxation?

Both companies and LLPs are taxed as separate entities, and the applicable rates, surcharges and treatment of profit distribution differ. Tax rules change with each Finance Act, and India's new Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026, so the better choice for tax depends on your expected profits, how you plan to take money out of the business, and current law. Discuss your numbers with a tax advisor before deciding on tax grounds alone.

Can I switch later?

Yes. A private company can be converted into an LLP, and an LLP can be converted into a company, subject to the conditions in the law. Conversion takes time and paperwork, so it is better to choose the right structure at the start.

Startup India recognition

Both private limited companies and LLPs can apply for DPIIT Startup India recognition if they meet the eligibility conditions: generally, being up to 10 years old, with turnover not exceeding ₹100 crore in any financial year, and working towards innovation or a scalable business model. Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper to maintain, a company or an LLP?

An LLP usually has lower ongoing compliance costs, mainly because a statutory audit and several company filings are not required for smaller LLPs.

Can a single person form a company?

Yes, through a One Person Company (OPC), which needs one member who is a natural person and an Indian citizen, plus a nominee. Learn about OPC registration →

Do I need a registered office?

Yes. Both a company and an LLP need a registered office address in India, supported by address proof and an NOC from the owner.
