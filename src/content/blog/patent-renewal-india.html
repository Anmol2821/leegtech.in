A granted patent in India stays in force only if renewal fees are paid every year. Missing a payment can end the patent, and anyone may then use the invention. This guide explains when renewal fees fall due, what happens if a patent is granted late, the grace period, and how a lapsed patent can be restored, under the Patents Act, 1970 and the Patents Rules, 2003.

How long does a patent last?

The term of a patent is 20 years from the date of filing of the application (Section 53(1)). For a PCT application that entered the national phase in India, it is 20 years from the international filing date. Every patent is dated as of its filing date (Section 45(1)), which is why renewal years are counted from that date, not from the date of grant.

When are renewal fees due?

Under rule 80(1), renewal fees are payable at the end of the second year from the date of the patent and of every year after that, and must reach the Patent Office before the year in question expires. In practice, the first renewal fee is for the third year, and fees are then due every year up to the twentieth.

When paying, quote the patent number, its date and the year being paid for (rule 80(2)). The Controller credits the fee and issues a certificate that it has been paid (rule 80(4)).

Paying in advance

Renewal fees for two or more years may be paid in advance (rule 80(3)). Since the Patents (Amendment) Rules, 2024, a ten per cent reduction applies where renewal fees are paid in advance through electronic mode for at least four years.

What if the patent is granted after several years?

Examination often takes more than two years, so several renewal years may have passed by the time a patent is granted. Under Section 142(4), the fees that have become due in the meantime may be paid within three months from the date the patent is recorded in the register, or within an extended period of not more than nine months from that date.

The six-month extension

If a renewal fee is not paid on time, the period can be extended by up to six months on a request in Form 4 with the prescribed fee (rule 80(1A)). Section 53(2) provides that a patent ceases to have effect if the renewal fee is not paid within the prescribed period or the extended period.

What happens if the fee is still not paid?

The patent ceases to have effect. Under Section 53(4), once a patent ceases for non-payment of renewal fees, the subject matter it covered is no longer entitled to any protection.

Restoring a lapsed patent

  • Time limit: an application for restoration can be made within 18 months from the date the patent ceased to have effect (Section 60(1)), in Form 15 (rule 84(1)).
  • Who can apply: the patentee or their legal representative; for jointly held patents, one or more of the holders may apply with the Controller's leave.
  • What must be shown: a verified statement fully setting out the circumstances of the failure to pay (Section 60(3)). The Controller must be prima facie satisfied that the failure was unintentional and that there was no undue delay in applying (Section 61(1)).
  • Opposition: if the Controller is satisfied, the application is published, and any person interested may oppose it within two months of publication (rule 85) on the ground that the failure was not unintentional or that there was undue delay.
  • Rights after restoration: a restored patent may be subject to conditions protecting people who started using the invention while it had lapsed (Section 62).

Restoration is discretionary and not automatic, so it is far better to keep renewals on time.

Practical tips

  • Note each renewal date from the filing date, with reminders well before the due date.
  • After grant, check straight away whether earlier renewal years have become payable.
  • Review the portfolio each year and decide which patents are still worth renewing.
  • Keep the payment certificates with your patent records.

Frequently asked questions

Do I pay renewal fees while the application is still pending?

Renewal fees become payable only once the patent is granted. Fees for the years that have already passed then fall due under Section 142(4).

Is there a fee for the first two years?

No renewal fee is payable for the first two years; the first renewal fee is for the third year.

Can a lapsed patent always be restored?

No. The application must be made within 18 months, and the Controller must be satisfied that the failure was unintentional and that there was no undue delay. It can also be opposed.
