If you have an invention that is still being refined, or you need to talk about it soon, a provisional patent application lets you secure an early filing date without waiting until every detail is final. This guide explains how it works under the Patents Act, 1970, what it must contain, and when it is the right choice.

What is a provisional application?

Under Section 9 of the Patents Act, 1970, a patent application can be filed with either a provisional specification or a complete specification. A provisional specification describes the invention but does not need claims. It is followed by a complete specification, which describes the invention fully and ends with the claims that define the scope of protection.

The main benefit is the priority date. Claims in the later complete specification that are fairly based on the matter disclosed in the provisional get the provisional's filing date as their priority date (Section 11). In a first-to-file system, that earlier date can be decisive.

The 12-month deadline

The complete specification must be filed within 12 months from the date of filing the provisional application (Section 9(1)). If it is not, the application is treated as abandoned. This period cannot be extended, so it should be diarised from day one. You can work out your dates with our free Patent Deadline Calculator.

The same 12-month window is also the deadline for claiming priority in other countries, through the Paris Convention or a PCT application.

When does filing a provisional make sense?

  • You need to disclose soon: before an investor pitch, exhibition, conference or publication.
  • Development is ongoing: the core idea is clear, but you are still testing variants.
  • You want time to assess value: the 12 months can be used to test the market or raise funds before investing in full drafting and international filings.
  • Several related improvements are expected: where provisionals are filed for cognate (closely related) inventions, a single complete specification may cover them, subject to the conditions in Section 9(2).

If the invention is already fully developed, filing a complete specification directly may be simpler and faster.

What should a provisional specification contain?

A provisional is not a formality. Only what it actually discloses gets the early date, so it should describe:

  • the technical field and the problem the invention solves;
  • how the invention works, with its key components or steps;
  • the main embodiments and useful variations;
  • drawings or flowcharts where they help explain the invention.

A thin or vague provisional can leave later claims without support, which means they would only get the later complete-specification date.

What happens after filing?

  1. You receive an application number and filing date.
  2. Within 12 months, the complete specification is filed with claims.
  3. The application is published, normally 18 months from the priority date, or earlier on request.
  4. A request for examination is filed within the prescribed time, and the application then moves through examination.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Treating the provisional as a one-paragraph summary.
  • Missing the 12-month deadline for the complete specification.
  • Publicly disclosing details that were not included in the provisional.
  • Filing abroad first without satisfying Section 39 (foreign filing permission) as a resident of India.

Frequently asked questions

Is a provisional application examined?

No. Examination happens only after the complete specification is filed and a request for examination is made.

Can I describe my product as "patent pending" after filing a provisional?

You can say that a patent has been applied for. Do not describe it as patented until a patent is granted.

Can I add new features in the complete specification?

You can, but new matter that was not disclosed in the provisional does not get the provisional's priority date.
