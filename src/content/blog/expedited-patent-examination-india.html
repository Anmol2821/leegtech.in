Ordinary patent examination in India can take a long time. For certain applicants, rule 24C of the Patents Rules, 2003 provides expedited examination, with shorter time limits for the Patent Office at each stage. This guide explains who is eligible, how to apply and what changes once the request is accepted.

Who is eligible?

Under rule 24C(1), a request for expedited examination can be filed on any of these grounds:

  • India was indicated as the competent International Searching Authority, or elected as the International Preliminary Examining Authority, in the corresponding international (PCT) application;
  • the applicant is a startup;
  • the applicant is a small entity;
  • the applicant is a natural person and female, or, where all joint applicants are natural persons, at least one of them is female;
  • the applicant is a department of the Government;
  • the applicant is an institution established by a Central, Provincial or State Act that is owned or controlled by the Government;
  • the applicant is a Government company under Section 2(45) of the Companies Act, 2013;
  • the applicant is an institution wholly or substantially financed by the Government;
  • the application relates to a sector notified by the Central Government on a request from the head of a Central Government department, after inviting public comments;
  • the applicant is eligible under an arrangement between the Indian Patent Office and a foreign patent office for processing patent applications.

Startups and small entities need the relevant recognition or proof of status. Rule 24C(5) adds that a request filed by a startup or small entity will not be questioned merely because it later ceases to be a startup or small entity after filing the application, for example because its recognition period lapses or its turnover crosses the threshold.

How to request expedited examination

  • File Form 18A with the prescribed fee, electronically only, within the period for requesting examination under rule 24B.
  • If the application has not yet been published, and no request for early publication has been filed, Form 18A must be accompanied by a request for publication under rule 24A (rule 24C(3)).
  • An ordinary request for examination already filed can be converted into an expedited request by paying the relevant fee and filing the supporting documents (rule 24C(2)).

If the request does not meet the requirements, it is processed as an ordinary request for examination under rule 24B, and the applicant is informed (rule 24C(4)).

For applications filed after the Patents (Amendment) Rules, 2024 came into force, the period for requesting examination is 31 months from the priority date or the date of filing, whichever is earlier. What changes once the request is accepted?

StageTime limit under rule 24C
Examiner's reportOrdinarily one month, not more than two months, from the reference to the examiner
Controller's disposal of the examiner's reportOne month from receiving the report
First statement of objections (FER) issuedWithin fifteen days of that disposal
Putting the application in order for grantSix months from the FER, extendable by three months on a Form 4 request filed in time
Disposal of the application by the ControllerWithin three months of the last reply, or of the last date to put the application in order, whichever is earlier (not applicable where there is a pre-grant opposition)

These are time limits for the Patent Office, not a promise of grant. The application is examined against the same legal standards as any other, and the Controller may limit the number of expedited requests accepted in a year (rule 24C(13)).

Points to consider before filing

  • Be ready to respond quickly. Objections still have to be answered, and the timeline moves faster.
  • Get the specification right first. A strong prior art search and careful claim drafting matter more under a compressed timeline.
  • Early publication is part of the package. Expedited examination requires the application to be published, so the invention becomes public sooner.
  • Check eligibility documents early. Keep startup recognition or small entity evidence ready when filing.

Frequently asked questions

Can a large company use expedited examination?

Only if it qualifies on another ground in rule 24C(1), for example choosing India as the ISA or IPEA in its PCT application, or a patent office arrangement that applies to it.

Does expedited examination mean my patent will be granted?

No. It only shortens the processing time. Grant depends on the application meeting all requirements of the Patents Act.

Is the FER deadline different under expedited examination?

No. It is six months from the FER, extendable by three months, the same as ordinary examination. See our guide on how to respond to an FER.
