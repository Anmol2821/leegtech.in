Founders often ask whether they need a patent, a trademark, a copyright or a design registration. The honest answer is that these rights protect different things, and a single product frequently needs more than one of them. This guide explains each right under Indian law in plain language and shows how they fit together.

The short answer

Patent: protecting how it works

A patent protects an invention, which is a new product or process that involves an inventive step and is capable of industrial application. It gives the owner the right to stop others from making, using, selling or importing the patented invention in India without permission.

Some subject matter is excluded under Sections 3 and 4 of the Patents Act, including mere discoveries, abstract theories, business methods, and computer programmes per se. Patents must be applied for, examined and granted. Timing is critical: a public disclosure before filing can destroy novelty.

Trademark: protecting who you are

A trademark distinguishes your goods or services from those of others. It can be a word, logo, slogan, shape, colour combination or other sign capable of graphical representation. Registration gives you exclusive rights for the registered goods or services and makes it much easier to stop copycats.

You may use ™ once you claim a mark. The ® symbol is reserved for registered marks; falsely representing a mark as registered is an offence under the Act.

Copyright: protecting your creative expression

Copyright protects original literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works, cinematograph films and sound recordings. Software is protected as a literary work. Copyright arises automatically on creation, but registration with the Copyright Office gives you prima facie evidence of the particulars entered in the Register of Copyrights, which is valuable in disputes and transactions.

Copyright protects the expression of an idea, not the idea itself. Two people can independently write different code that achieves the same function.

Design: protecting how it looks

Design registration protects the features of shape, configuration, pattern, ornamentation or composition of lines or colours applied to an article, which appeal to the eye in the finished article. It does not protect how the product functions; that is the role of patents.

The design must be new or original and not previously published in India or elsewhere. Register before you launch, exhibit or list the product online.

One product, several rights: an example

Imagine a startup launching a smart water bottle with a companion app:

  • Patent: the new mechanism that tracks water intake inside the bottle.
  • Design: the distinctive shape of the bottle and cap.
  • Trademark: the brand name and logo printed on the bottle and app.
  • Copyright: the app's code, user interface artwork, and marketing content.

Each right covers a different layer of value. Relying on only one leaves the other layers open to copying.

Which should you file first?

  1. Before any public disclosure: file patent and design applications, because prior publication can destroy novelty.
  2. Before launch or marketing spend: run a trademark search and file the trademark, so your brand investment is protected.
  3. Alongside development: make sure employees and vendors assign IP to your company in writing, and register key copyright works.

Frequently asked questions

Can I protect an idea?

Not as such. Ideas become protectable when they take a protectable form: a technical invention (patent), a creative expression (copyright), a product appearance (design) or a brand (trademark). Until then, use NDAs and confidentiality.

Is one registration valid worldwide?

No. IP rights are territorial. International routes such as the PCT for patents and the Madrid Protocol for trademarks make it easier to extend protection, but rights are still granted country by country.
