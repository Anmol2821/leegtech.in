Section 39, Form 25 and the consequences of getting it wrong. category: patent ---

If you live in India and want to patent your invention abroad, there is one rule to get right before you file anywhere outside India: Section 39 of the Patents Act, 1970. Getting it wrong can cost you the patent in India and carries penalties. This guide explains when permission is needed and how to get it.

What Section 39 requires

Under Section 39(1), no person resident in India may make, or cause to be made, an application outside India for a patent for an invention unless either:

  • they have a written permit from the Controller, or
  • an application for the same invention was filed in India at least six weeks earlier, and either no secrecy direction under Section 35 was given on it or all such directions have been revoked.

The rule applies to "persons resident in India". It does not apply where an application for the invention was first filed in a country outside India by a person resident outside India (Section 39(3)).

Why the rule exists

Section 35 allows the Controller to issue secrecy directions for inventions relevant to defence. The six-week gap, or the permit, gives the Patent Office time to check whether an invention should not be disclosed abroad. Where the invention is relevant for defence purposes or atomic energy, the Controller cannot grant a permit without the prior consent of the Central Government (proviso to Section 39(2)).

The two routes

Route 1: file in India first and wait six weeks

This is the most common route. You file in India, often a provisional application, and file abroad or file a PCT application only after six weeks have passed, provided no secrecy direction has been issued. You still have the 12-month priority window, so the six weeks rarely cause a problem.

Route 2: request a permit in Form 25

If you need to file abroad first, or within six weeks of the Indian filing, request permission in Form 25 (rule 71(1)). The Controller must dispose of the request within 21 days of filing. For inventions relating to defence or atomic energy, the 21 days run from the date the Central Government's consent is received (rule 71(2)). The request should describe the invention enough for it to be assessed.

What happens if you contravene Section 39

  • Indian application: the application for a patent in India is deemed to have been abandoned (Section 40).
  • Indian patent: any patent granted is liable to be revoked (Section 40, read with Section 64(1)(n)).
  • Penalty: a person who applies in contravention of Section 39 is punishable with imprisonment of up to two years, or a fine, or both (Section 118).

Common situations

  • Indian inventor working for a foreign company: Section 39 covers anyone resident in India who makes "or causes to be made" an application abroad. Where an inventor is resident in India, check Section 39 before the foreign filing, even if the applicant is a foreign company; a Form 25 permit removes doubt.
  • Joint inventors in India and abroad: check the rule before the first filing anywhere; a permit is often the safer choice.
  • Filing a PCT application with a foreign receiving office: this is an application outside India, so Section 39 applies.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit if I file in India first?

Not if you wait at least six weeks after the Indian filing before filing abroad and no secrecy direction applies (or all have been revoked).

How long does a permit take?

Rule 71(2) requires the Controller to dispose of the request within 21 days of filing, except for defence or atomic energy inventions, where the period runs from the Central Government's consent.

Does Section 39 apply to foreign nationals living in India?

The rule applies to persons resident in India. Residence, not nationality, is what matters, so foreign nationals resident in India should check it before filing abroad.
