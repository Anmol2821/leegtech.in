What is Response to First Examination Report?

After a request for examination, the Indian Patent Office issues a First Examination Report listing objections. Typical objections cover lack of novelty or inventive step against cited documents, non-patentable subject matter under Section 3, unclear claims, insufficient disclosure and formal requirements.

The applicant has six months from the date of the FER to put the application in order for grant. This can be extended by up to three months on request. If the deadline is missed, the application is treated as abandoned. We analyse each objection and the cited prior art, and respond with technical and legal arguments and, where needed, claim amendments that stay within the original disclosure.

Frequently asked questions

Can I change my claims in the FER reply?

Yes, within limits. Amendments must be supported by what was originally disclosed and must not broaden the scope of the invention.

What if the examiner is not convinced?

The Controller usually issues a hearing notice, where the arguments can be presented in person or by video conference.

Can you reply if another firm filed my application?

Yes. We can take over the matter by filing a fresh power of attorney.
