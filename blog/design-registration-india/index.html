Design Registration in India: Process, Term and Documents

By Anmol Shrivastava, Founder, Leegtech · Published 27 November 2026 · 4 min read

If your product stands out because of how it looks, its shape, pattern or ornamentation, design registration can protect that appearance against copying. This guide explains what can be registered under the Designs Act, 2000, the documents you need, how the process works and how long protection lasts.

What is a "design"?

Section 2(d) defines a design as only the features of shape, configuration, pattern, ornament or composition of lines or colours applied to any article, in two or three dimensions or both, by any industrial process or means, which in the finished article appeal to and are judged solely by the eye.

It does not include:

  • any mode or principle of construction, or anything that is in substance a mere mechanical device;
  • a trade mark or property mark;
  • an artistic work as defined in the Copyright Act, 1957.

In short, a design protects how a product looks, not how it works. How it works may be protected by a patent; our article on patents, trademarks, copyright and designs compares them.

Which designs can be registered?

Under Section 4, a design cannot be registered if it:

  • is not new or original;
  • has been disclosed to the public anywhere in India or in any other country, by publication in tangible form, by use or in any other way, before the filing date (or priority date, where applicable);
  • is not significantly distinguishable from known designs or combinations of known designs; or
  • contains scandalous or obscene matter.

The Controller may also refuse a design whose use would be contrary to public order or morality. Because novelty is judged worldwide, file before you launch, exhibit or post the product online.

Documents and information needed

  • Application in Form 1, with the applicant's name, address and nationality, the name of the article and its class;
  • representation sheets: clear drawings or images of the article from the relevant views;
  • a statement of novelty, identifying the features of the design that are new, and any disclaimer (for example, of mechanical action, trade marks, words, letters or numerals);
  • a power of attorney, if filed through an agent (it generally needs to be stamped under the applicable state law; see our stamp duty tool);
  • a priority document, if claiming priority from a convention country.

Classification

A design is registered in respect of articles in a class (Section 5(3) and Section 6). India follows a classification based on the Locarno Classification. Choosing the right class and naming the article correctly is important, because protection is tied to them.

The registration process

  1. Filing: file Form 1 with the representation sheets and the official fee. The filing date matters, because a registered design is registered as of the date of application (Section 5(6)).
  2. Examination: the Controller refers the application to an examiner, who reports on whether the design can be registered under the Act and Rules (proviso to Section 5(1)).
  3. Objections: if objections are raised, the applicant responds, amends or asks for a hearing. An application not completed within the prescribed time because of the applicant's default is deemed abandoned (Section 5(5)).
  4. Registration and publication: once accepted, the design is registered and its particulars are published, after which it is open to public inspection (Section 7).

If the Controller refuses registration, an appeal lies to the High Court within three months of the order (Sections 5(4) and 36).

Priority from abroad

If you first applied for the design in a convention country, you can claim priority in India if the Indian application is made within six months of that application (Section 44).

How long does protection last?

  • Initial term: 10 years from the date of registration (Section 11(1)).
  • Extension: one further period of 5 years, if an application for extension is made with the prescribed fee before the 10 years expire (Section 11(2)).
  • Restoration: if a design lapses because the extension fee was not paid, the proprietor may apply for restoration within one year from the date it ceased to have effect (Section 12).

What registration gives you

During the term, others may not, without the proprietor's licence or written consent, apply the design or a fraudulent or obvious imitation of it to articles in the registered class for sale, or import such articles for sale (Section 22, "piracy of registered design"). Any person interested can also petition to cancel a registration on the grounds in Section 19, for example that the design was not new or was published earlier.

Frequently asked questions

Can I register a design after I have started selling the product?

Usually not. Prior disclosure to the public anywhere, including by sale or online posting, generally destroys novelty.

Can a logo be registered as a design?

No. Trade marks are excluded from the definition of design; protect a logo through trademark registration.

Can a functional feature be protected as a design?

No. A mode or principle of construction, or a mere mechanical device, is excluded. Consider a patent instead.
