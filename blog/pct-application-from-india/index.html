PCT Application from India: Timeline and National Phase Explained

By Anmol Shrivastava, Founder, Leegtech · Published 24 November 2026 · 4 min read

If you want patent protection in more than one country, the Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT) lets you start with a single international application and decide later which countries to pursue. India is a member of the PCT. This guide explains the steps and key dates for applicants filing from India, under the PCT, the Patents Act, 1970 and the Patents Rules, 2003.

What a PCT application is, and what it is not

A PCT application is one international application that can later be taken into the member countries you choose. There is no "international patent": each country grants or refuses the patent under its own law during the national phase. What the PCT gives you is a single filing date in all member countries, an international search, and more time to decide where to invest.

Step 1: the Indian filing and the 12-month window

Most Indian applicants first file a patent application in India, often a provisional application. Its filing date becomes the priority date. The PCT application claiming that priority should be filed within 12 months of it, the same window that applies to Paris Convention filings.

Residents of India must also respect Section 39. A resident may apply for a patent outside India only with a written permit from the Controller, unless an application for the same invention was filed in India at least six weeks earlier and no secrecy direction under Section 35 applies (or all such directions have been revoked).

Step 2: filing the international application

An Indian applicant can file the international application with the Indian Patent Office acting as receiving office, in English or Hindi (rules 18 and 19). The appropriate office keeps a home copy and sends the record copy to the International Bureau of WIPO and the search copy to the chosen International Searching Authority. If the application is to be filed with another receiving office, such as the International Bureau, check Section 39 first.

Step 3: international search and written opinion

An International Searching Authority (ISA) issues an international search report listing the most relevant prior art, with a written opinion on whether the claims appear novel, inventive and industrially applicable. The Indian Patent Office acts as an ISA (ISA/IN) under rule 19A, so Indian applicants can choose it where it is competent. Choosing India as the ISA, or electing it as the International Preliminary Examining Authority, is also one of the grounds for expedited examination in India.

Step 4: international publication

The international application is published by WIPO promptly after 18 months from the priority date, unless the applicant asks for earlier publication or the application is withdrawn first.

Step 5 (optional): international preliminary examination

Under Chapter II of the PCT, the applicant can file a demand for international preliminary examination, which gives an opportunity to amend and argue before national phase. This is optional; many applicants go straight to national phase.

Step 6: national phase in India

  • Deadline: 31 months from the priority date (rule 20(4)(i)). The Indian Patent Office does not start processing before then unless the applicant expressly asks for earlier processing (rule 20(4)(ii)).
  • Form: the national phase application is made in Form 1 under Section 7(1A) (rule 20(1)), with the national fee.
  • Translation: if the international application was not filed or published in English, a verified English translation must be filed (rule 20(3) and (5)).
  • Priority document: if it was not furnished during the international phase as the PCT regulations require, it must be filed before the 31-month deadline, or the Office will invite it within three months; otherwise the priority claim is disregarded (rule 21).
  • Filing date and term: the filing date in India is the international filing date (Section 7(1B)), and the patent term, if granted, is 20 years from the international filing date (Explanation to Section 53(1)).

Treat 31 months as a firm deadline. Most other countries have national phase deadlines of 30 or 31 months, but each country's rules must be checked separately. Our Patent Deadline Calculator shows the Indian dates from a priority date.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a PCT application if I only want protection in India?

No. A national application in India is enough. The PCT is useful when you may want protection in several countries.

Can I enter India's national phase after 31 months?

Do not plan on it. Treat 31 months from the priority date as the deadline. Any relief for a missed deadline is limited and at the Controller's discretion, and missing it can end the application's route into India.

Is the international search report binding on national offices?

No. It is not binding, but national examiners usually take it into account.
